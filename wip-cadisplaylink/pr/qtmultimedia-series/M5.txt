QVideoWindow: Let the display refresh at the video's frame rate

On a display with a variable refresh rate, like ProMotion, the panel
refreshes at its maximum rate unless the windows ask for less, even
when a video window only shows a new frame 24 times per second.

On such displays, while a media player plays, set
QWindow::preferredFrameRate on the video window to the rate its frames
arrive at, the stream's frame rate times the playback rate, but only
when the display shows that rate with an even cadence, every frame for
the same number of refreshes, like 24 fps on a 120 Hz display. Other
rates are paced at the next faster exact rate: 25 fps at 30 Hz would
show every fifth frame for twice as long as the others, more unevenly
than refreshing at the full rate, so they get no preference, as before.
A whole multiple of the rate counts too, and rates up to 0.2% slower
than an exact one, like 23.976 and 29.97, but not faster ones, whose
frames would be dropped. qVideoPreferredFrameRate() computes
it from the refresh rate of the window's screen, for every frame, and
the preference is reset once it no longer applies, so it follows the
window to other screens.

The price is latency: alone on its display, the window is then shown
on the grid of that rate, so a frame may wait up to one frame interval
for it, instead of up to one refresh, and a frame arriving close to a
tick may slip to the next one. 23.976 and 29.97 fps content shows about
one frame in 1000 twice as long (every 42 and 33 seconds). That's why
displays with a fixed refresh rate get no preference, as it would only
delay frames there, and why a paused player's frames, which arrive when
seeking, get none either.

Only on macOS, where the preference paces update requests to the
display (with vertical sync) and NSScreen tells a display with a
variable refresh rate. iOS paces too, but doesn't tell. On the other
platforms the preference is a minimum interval from the previous
update request, which could only make frames late, as the window
requests one update per frame anyway. And only for a media
player's frames: cameras report the maximum rate of their format but
deliver fewer frames in low light, and screen captures deliver on
changes. A preference that the window didn't set itself is neither
replaced nor reset (unless it's the same value).

QVideoWidget shows its video in a QVideoWindow, and so gets it too; its
documentation says so. QVideoSink gets a private accessor for its
source, the media player. Variable frame rate content reports its
average rate, so faster segments may be paced below their rate.

Requires QWindow::preferredFrameRate (Qt 6.13).
