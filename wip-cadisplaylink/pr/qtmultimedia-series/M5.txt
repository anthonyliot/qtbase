QVideoWindow: Show frames evenly on variable refresh rate displays

On a display with a variable refresh rate, like ProMotion, the window
renders a frame at the first display link callback after it arrives,
and the frame is shown at the first refresh after that. So a frame
arriving a little early or late is shown a refresh earlier or later,
and 24 fps video shows some frames for 33 or 50 ms instead of 41.7.
With the FFmpeg backend on a 120 Hz ProMotion panel, 79% to 94% of the
window's updates were 41.7 ms apart, fewer in stretches and while
another window updated the display at 120 Hz, against 98% to 100% with
this change; on screen, in the one undisturbed pair, 88% of the frames
were shown for 41.7 ms, against 97%. The AVFoundation backend's frames
follow the display, and were about as even without it. The panel
itself doesn't need the preference to slow down: with FFmpeg it
refreshed at 24 Hz most of the time without it.

On such displays, while a media player plays, set
QWindow::preferredFrameRate on the video window to the rate its frames
arrive at, the stream's frame rate times the playback rate, but only
when the display shows that rate with an even cadence, every frame for
the same number of refreshes, like 24 fps on a 120 Hz display. The
window is then updated on the grid of that rate, and frames aren't
shown a refresh early or late. Other rates are paced at the next faster
exact rate: 25 fps at 30 Hz would show every fifth frame for twice as
long as the others, more unevenly than refreshing at the full rate, so
they get no preference, as before. A whole multiple of the rate counts
too, and rates up to 0.2% slower than an exact one, like 23.976 and
29.97, but not faster ones, whose frames would be dropped.
qVideoPreferredFrameRate() computes it from the refresh rate of the
window's screen, for every frame, and the preference is reset once it
no longer applies, so it follows the window to other screens.

The price is latency: a frame waits for the next tick of that grid, up
to one frame interval, instead of up to one refresh, and a frame that
arrives after its tick waits for the next one, while the previous frame
stays on screen for another frame interval (in one run under a heavy
load, 7.6% of the updates, against 0.3% without). Measured from the
update request to its delivery, with 24 fps on the 120 Hz panel: a
median of 5 to 33 ms with FFmpeg, depending on where the frames fall on
the grid, against 3 to 6 ms without this change, and 17 to 33 ms against
9 ms with AVFoundation; from commit to display nothing changes. 23.976
and 29.97 fps content shows about one frame in 1000 twice as long
(every 42 and 33 seconds). That's why displays with a fixed refresh
rate get no preference, as it would only delay frames there, and why a
paused player's frames, which arrive when seeking, get none either.

Only on macOS, where the preference paces update requests to the
display (with vertical sync) and NSScreen tells a display with a
variable refresh rate. iOS paces too, but doesn't tell. On the other
platforms the preference is a minimum interval from the previous
update request, which could only make frames late, as the window
requests one update per frame anyway. And only for a media
player's frames: cameras report the maximum rate of their format but
deliver fewer frames in low light, and screen captures deliver on
changes. A preference that the window didn't set itself is neither
replaced nor reset (unless it's the same value).

QVideoWidget shows its video in a QVideoWindow, and so gets it too; its
documentation says so. QVideoSink gets a private accessor for its
source, the media player. Variable frame rate content reports its
average rate, so faster segments may be paced below their rate.

Requires QWindow::preferredFrameRate (Qt 6.13).
